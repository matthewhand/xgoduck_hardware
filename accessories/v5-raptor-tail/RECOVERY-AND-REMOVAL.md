# Recovering with the 167 mm curved tail

At a 12° forward-trunk target, the original curved tail failed to recover from a 90° right-side start. Narrowing its proximal shell by 10% and adding two low rounded contact shoes gave the passive hinge space to roll clear. The same shipped get-up and walk policies got upright in 3.28 s and advanced 2.46 m after recovery. The original-rail revision also passed at 80°, 85°, 95° and 100°. The visual replay shows the original and revised geometry side by side. The walk-away metric is measured along the heading at resumption, excluding the initial fall.

The 12° demand gave roughly 8.5° measured forward lean. In the integer-degree standing sweep from 8° to 30°, 17° was the highest passing command, giving roughly 14.75° measured and 1.59 m traveled. It also passed following a right-side start. Higher targets through 30° failed the standing-walk criteria. This is a limit for this geometry, policy and test.

The alternate removable rail cartridges change the installed inertia and contacts. Their separate 90° right-side test recovered in 3.30 s and walked 2.316 m. With the collar, both cartridges and tail removed, the recessed receiver passed four of four shipped forward-roll trials. Leaving the older exposed rails on the duck passed zero of four. The necklace arm study also tested longer tails. The arms added about 5.7 g and did not establish an advantage that would justify a longer tail for running.
